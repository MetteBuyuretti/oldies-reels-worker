OLDIES RADYO • VOICES OF THE SIXTIES • ITALY

10 Famous Italian Singers and Bands of the 1960s

Sanremo • Cantautori • Television pop • Beat

Italy entered the sixties with melodic song, radio orchestras and the prestige of Sanremo. Domenico Modugno’s late-fifties breakthrough had already demonstrated that established performance conventions could change.

PART 1 • ROOTS

The legacy of the 1950s

RAI television, festivals and musical films carried singers into ordinary homes. Seven-inch records, live performance and screen personality reinforced one another.

PART 2 • CHANGE

The sixties begin

American rock and roll and British beat brought new energy, while the cantautori made songwriting more personal. Interpreters such as Mina transformed composers’ material through a commanding vocal identity.

These ten artists connect festival victories, teenage excitement and songwriting. The listening route stays with 1960–1969 recordings rather than borrowing later hits to make the decade look familiar.

PART 3 • PORTRAITS

Ten essential voices of the sixties

♫
01

Mina

Vocal pop / television

Mina connected the energy of early rock-influenced pop to sophisticated songwriting and exceptional vocal control. Il cielo in una stanza, written by Gino Paoli, and Se telefonando, with Ennio Morricone’s music, offer contrasting landmarks. RAI’s preserved Studio Uno performances show how voice and television presence worked together. She belongs at the centre of this decade because interpretation itself becomes the creative event, not merely the delivery of somebody else’s composition.

Sixties listening notes: Il cielo in una stanza — 1960 • Se telefonando — 1966
♫
02

Adriano Celentano

Rock ’n’ roll / songwriter

Celentano’s physical performance style helped give Italian youth pop a recognisable rock-and-roll figure. The 1961 Sanremo period around 24.000 baci belongs to that arrival, while Il ragazzo della via Gluck in 1966 introduces a more reflective story about a changing neighbourhood. RAI’s history of the festival places him among the young performers reshaping its sound. The contrast between the two records captures a decade of widening ambition.

Sixties listening notes: 24.000 baci — 1961 • Il ragazzo della via Gluck — 1966
♫
03

Gianni Morandi

Teen pop / musicarelli

Morandi’s arrival in 1962 combined records with the television showcase Alta pressione. His youthful, open presentation made him a natural figure for the musical films known as musicarelli. Fatti mandare dalla mamma captures the immediacy of that early repertoire. Rather than read his long later career backwards, listen to the concise, energetic recordings that established a teenage star in a rapidly expanding Italian screen-and-record market.

In 1962, Alta pressione and a televised meeting with Neil Sedaka brought Andavo a 100 all’ora to viewers.

Sixties listening notes: Fatti mandare dalla mamma — 1962
♫
04

Patty Pravo

Beat-era pop / vocal identity

Pravo emerged from Rome’s Piper club culture with a cool, unusual vocal presence. Ragazzo triste introduced her recording persona in 1966; La bambola made that identity widely familiar in 1968. Her importance is not simply that she looked contemporary, but that the voice suggested independence and tension within accessible pop songs. She offers a different female-star model from the festival singer or the reassuring screen sweetheart.

Sixties listening notes: Ragazzo triste — 1966 • La bambola — 1968
05

Gigliola Cinquetti

Sanremo / Eurovision

Cinquetti won Sanremo and then Eurovision with Non ho l’età in 1964, bringing Italy its first Eurovision victory. Her understated presentation offered a striking contrast to the more physical youth-pop performers. The restrained vocal and romantic lyric made the song easy to carry across languages. That international exposure explains her place in a sixties selection without requiring later contests or later chart successes to support it.

Sixties listening notes: Non ho l’età — 1964
♫
06

Ornella Vanoni

Interpretive pop / chanson

Vanoni’s sixties moved beyond the theatrical songs associated with her beginnings towards a broader interpretive pop repertoire. Senza fine connects her to Gino Paoli’s songwriting, while La musica è finita belongs to the 1967 Sanremo period. Her vocal personality gives the selection a mature, conversational intensity. RAI’s archive helps distinguish that gradual transformation from a story told only through L’appuntamento, which falls outside the decade.

Sixties listening notes: Senza fine — 1961 • La musica è finita — 1967
♫
07

Milva

Festival pop / dramatic interpretation

Milva’s 1961 Sanremo debut with Il mare nel cassetto introduced a substantial, dramatic voice within the new generation of festival performers. She also developed connections to stage repertoire that made her more than a conventional teenage idol. Her sixties recordings should be heard before the later theatrical and international work by which some listeners know her. The contrast between vocal gravity and accessible melody is the useful starting point.

Il mare nel cassetto (1961), Cetra SP 977: her first Sanremo appearance finished third, paired with Gino Latilla.

Sixties listening notes: Il mare nel cassetto — 1961
♫
08

Gino Paoli

Cantautore / intimate song

Paoli helped establish a songwriter’s intimacy within mainstream Italian song. Sapore di sale places that understated voice inside a beautifully economical pop record, while his writing for Mina and Vanoni expands the story beyond his own recordings. His importance is the circulation of an authorial sensibility among different interpreters. Listen for the conversational phrasing and emotional ambiguity that distinguish this repertoire from a purely demonstrative festival performance.

Sixties listening notes: Sapore di sale — 1963
♫
09

Luigi Tenco

Cantautore / reflective pop

Tenco’s recordings brought a reserved, questioning personality to Italian popular song. Mi sono innamorato di te is a clear entry into that 1962 voice. His death during the 1967 Sanremo festival became a major rupture in the event’s history, but it should not replace listening to the music. The songs’ economical language and unshowy delivery help explain why his work remained important to later singer-songwriters.

Sixties listening notes: Mi sono innamorato di te — 1962
♫
10

Domenico Modugno

Dramatic popular song / festival

Modugno’s worldwide Volare breakthrough belongs to 1958, but his festival presence continued in the sixties. Dio, come ti amo won Sanremo in 1966 with performances by Modugno and Gigliola Cinquetti, before he took it to Eurovision. That sequence shows the difference between domestic festival prestige and an international contest result. His emphatic interpretation provides continuity with the performer who had already challenged Italian song’s older stage conventions.

Sixties listening notes: Dio, come ti amo — 1966
